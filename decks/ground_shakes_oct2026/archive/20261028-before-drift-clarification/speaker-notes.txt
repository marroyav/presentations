Ground shakes — public review, 28 October 2026

The point of this presentation
We added tests of possible relationships. We did not discover the charge
precursor, opposite APA signs, disconnected-input response or electronics
pattern: these already appear in EDMS 3493627 v 1. We have not found the cause.
The strongest additional constraint is the charge-light mixing test: the
apparent timing peak survives replacing an event's PDS pulses with other
events' pulses. It therefore cannot identify a unique match by itself.

1. What this analysis adds
Say explicitly that the earlier slides are the starting point. The enlarged
charge sample is a cross-check. The new work measures pair timing, tests the
first-pulse clock and amplitude prediction, tests charge-light assignments,
and checks electrical fits on an unused run. The SC extension is conditional
on recorded shakes. These are additions to the evidence, not a diagnosed cause.

2. Three signals, three names
PDS means the photon-detection readout. A pulse on that readout need not be
light: voltage interference also produces pulses. The example uses one
connected input, channel 47 on DAPHNE 109, in run 32731 event 33.
The sharp candidates are at about -183 and -161 microseconds. The slow charge
precursor grows later in this example; its APA 1 two-ADC marker is -111.872
microseconds. The main shake is the large charge threshold used as time zero.
Top and middle are magnified pre-shake views. The lower trace uses the full
charge scale. Earlier does not by itself mean causal. The small sharp pulses,
the slow precursor and the main shake are separate measured features.

3. Double pulses
Event 33: 22.528 microseconds between selected pulses. Event 52: 65.536.
The survey found at least two selected multichannel candidates in 101/716
run 32731 records;80 have exactly two. Events with more than two give several
consecutive pairs,125 total. The denominator is selected local shake records,
not a complete detector exposure. Both times must be between -300 and-100
microseconds; pulse merging suppresses separations below about 10 microseconds.
Do not call these two confirmed optical flashes. They are also not the
post-main reflections shown in the earlier EDMS topology slide.

4. Relationship to the precursor and main event
First means first retained multichannel candidate in the selection window,
not necessarily the first pulse the detector produced. Readout gaps matter.
The left panel has two pulse-before-marker examples and one marker-before-
pulse example. The two-ADC crossing is a diagnostic marker, not an exact
start time. The right panel is the stronger population test: learn a fixed
pulse-to -charge delay on run 32731, then test it on 225 unused run 32739 records.
Its average error is 66.1 microseconds. A fixed phase relative to the known
main shake gives 33.4 microseconds. The first selected pulse is not a reliable
clock for the slow charge change. This does not prove it has no physical role.
Separately, early PDS features improve the slow-charge amplitude estimate by
only 4.1%. Main-shake clipping prevents interpreting ADC size as physical energy.

5. A charge-light assignment that fails
The coloured diagonal is a localized collection-wire signal, not the smooth
APA-average precursor. It is already observed at -331 microseconds. The two
selected PDS pulses follow at -183 and-161. Under a common-interaction-time
interpretation and the recorded timing, those later pulses cannot be parents
of the whole earlier feature. Matching only a late piece would be misleading.
This is an exploratory 2 D component, not a fitted 3 D track. Other later charge
features remain compatible candidates; relative electronics latency is not
independently calibrated. We do not conclude that there was no light.

6. The timing peak
For a particle interaction, prompt light can be followed by drifting charge.
We searched that delay. A broad maximum near 325 microseconds looked tempting.
We then kept each charge record and borrowed PDS pulse trains from other
events in the same run, file and assembly. The peak remained. The peak can
therefore arise from the common placement of features around the shake and
the usable recording window. It cannot locate the source or select a unique
light pulse. This comparison uses shake records; it is not a measurement of
the ordinary-background rate. Full 3 D matching is unfinished.

7. Electrical fit
Electrical pickup and the electronics-dependent pattern were already in
EDMS pp 21-23. The addition is a fit tested on an unused run and on the same
target samples with substituted reference signals. Same-event references
work better: relative root-squared error 0.918 versus 1.023. A value of 1 means
predicting no change. The effect is partial and varies strongly by channel.
It is not a fraction of light, a pure electrical subtraction, or proof of
which board or cable started the disturbance. Disconnections are reported
engineering facts; run-specific verification is still needed.

8. Slow controls
Do not say high voltage is unrelated to shakes. EDMS already reports HV
rate/amplitude trends and a preliminary wire-bias effect. Our new test asks
whether archived SC levels track three waveform measurements within the two
November runs. It removes file offsets and within-file linear time trends.
No eligible pair repeats at the predefined magnitude cutoff with one-minute
bins. A filtered-current hint appears only for one binning/value treatment.
This test does not establish zero correlation, test rate, or redo the bias-off
comparison. The direct 30201/30202 waveform-versus-bias comparison remains open.

9. What should follow
Use the specific questions from EDMS: quantify the wire-bias transition and
fit whole charge tracks. Add a matched sample without shakes to determine
which early features are distinctive. More galleries alone cannot answer these.
