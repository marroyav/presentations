How active is each physical module before the shake?

This combines readouts belonging to each module at the same recorded times. A module needs at least two recorded readouts. The bars follow actual physical order.

What does module activity measure?

For each module, measure how much its recorded waveforms fluctuate in each 0.512 µs piece. Take the middle value among the module’s recorded readouts, then over the precursor interval and across events. The unit is ADC counts. This measures recorded response, not calibrated light yield.

Each piece must contain all 32 native samples. Require at least two recorded inputs per module and 16 usable pieces, or 8.192 µs. An event must have at least four usable modules and twelve inputs with enough recording. Missing samples stay missing.

The precursor interval is −120 to −20 µs. Sharp-pulse intervals are omitted. The module profile has 49 usable APA1 events and 1,224 APA2 events; n beside a bar is that module’s event count.

Where is the largest signal at each stage?

Each bar counts events whose largest recorded signal is in that module. Use a stage button to compare pulse peaks, or Play to follow time from the main shake. These are snapshots, so fewer events may have data than in the wider precursor interval above.

The number of usable events changes with time. Missing samples do not mean inactivity. APA1 has only 8 usable events at −60 µs. A changing population histogram alone cannot show movement within an event.

What enters these histograms?

The signal size is the absolute distance from a fixed channel baseline. Each module takes its largest recorded channel value. An event needs at least 12 connected recorded channels across 4 modules, and a maximum of at least 20 ADC counts. Equal largest values in different modules are excluded. Each event counts once.

Early pulses are the first two existing sharp multichannel candidates earlier than −100 µs. Fixed snapshots span 0.512 µs. Module 8 in APA2 is excluded because its inputs are reported disconnected; that exclusion is not applied to APA1.

Does the response shift from the corner toward the centre?

APA2: in 377 of 411 events strongest in M1–M2 at −60 µs, the largest response is in M5–M7 at +10 µs. Each event is compared using the same channels at both times.

APA1: only 3 events support that comparison. We cannot establish or exclude the same spatial sequence. Neither result establishes a moving source.

Does one physical module start first?

Several modules often start together. APA1 has 14 early pulses with enough timed modules. Twelve have tied modules at the 20% pulse-depth threshold; all 14 have ties at 50%.

APA1 M1, M2 and M3 often belong to the earliest group. We do not find one module repeatedly starting alone before the others under both thresholds.

A module needs at least two timed readouts. Its start is the first measured crossing among them. Requiring the second crossing also produces many ties. This concerns sharp early pulses; the start of the slow precursor remains unresolved in APA1.

What can we say about movement?

The module that responds most strongly changes during the event. In APA2, the largest response commonly changes from the upper M1–M2 modules to M5–M7. APA1 has only three events with enough common recorded inputs to compare −60 and +10 µs. Its spatial sequence cannot yet be established or excluded.

Neither result establishes a travelling source. The two APAs were recorded in different runs, and the signal sizes and instrument delays are not calibrated.
