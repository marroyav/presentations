VD Detector Protection System — fast speaking script
I&I meeting, November 2026

28 main slides; 400 spoken words. Allow about 4 minutes including pauses.
Read one paragraph per slide. Backups 29–45 are for questions.

1. VD Detector Protection System

I’ll cover our starting point for the VD DPS, and the work needed for a full conceptual design.

2. The hardware connection list is still growing

We know rack protection and BDE. We still need the complete hardware connection list.

3. DPS protects equipment; SC operates and records

DPS protects equipment. Slow controls operates and records; local interlocks can act independently.

4. Facility inputs reach DPS through slow controls

We propose to bring facility status through slow controls to the DPS PLC.

5. The FFDG BMS connection needs an API licence

The Metasys interface was exercised with SBN data. Live FFDG access still needs the licence.

6. How FFDG BSI status can support detector protection

FFDG says support is next business day. Critical protection may need our own sensors.

7. The SDSTA SCADA interface needs a hardware survey

The SDSTA survey is identifying controllers and usable connections at Ross Dry.

8. SDSTA proposes a restricted SCADA status interface

SDSTA proposes status-only Modbus TCP. Variables, access controls and failure behaviour still need agreement.

9. Cryogenics presents one system to DUNE

Cryogenics keeps its own controls. We need agreed readiness states and responses to missing signals.

10. Trace each interlock from its source to the response

Trace each interlock from its source through the connection, decision, action and feedback.

11. Each group must identify its hardware connections

We need each consortium and LBNF to identify their connections, requirements, gaps and contact.

12. A trip removes the permit; recovery needs a new request

A trip removes the permit. Reset and re-enable are separate actions.

13. Build on the Batavia and SD DPS test setups

Batavia reports design and review progress. Our SD setup has hardware, Ignition, bridges and emulators; expansion is planned.

14. Detector

Now, the detector systems.

15. Ten BDE groups use the existing bottom-drift design

BDE provides the starting design: ten groups, eighty warm crates and dedicated supply inhibits.

16. Extend the BDE approach into a full DPS concept

We propose to extend that PLC and remote-I/O approach once the additional interfaces are mapped.

17. A cooling fault removes power from the affected crate

A fan fault inhibits power to the affected crate. Associated bias shutdown still needs agreement.

18. SC and DAQ use distinct DAPHNE service paths

SC and DAQ’s CCM use the OPC UA path; Hermes has a separate configuration interface.

19. DPS switches PoF power through the PDU

For PoF, the proposed shutdown uses the rack PDU. A separate box-enable path remains optional.

20. LV supplies power DAPHNE and PDS calibration boxes

For DAPHNE and calibration boxes, the working LV scope is thirty-four feeds; channel and inhibit assignments remain open.

21. Drift HV and bias supplies have separate inhibit paths

The HV design has separate wired inhibits for drift HV and CRP/field-cage bias.

22. Trace each protection input to a temperature sensor

Each protection measurement needs a sensor location, limit, response time and affected load.

23. Proposed laser rules block conflicting PDS requests

The proposed laser rule blocks conflicting PDS requests. Local laser-safety interlocks remain independent.

24. The purity-monitor inhibit rule still needs agreement

We still need to agree whether purity-monitor activity needs tagging or PDS bias inhibition.

25. Use rack sensors and PDUs for shared protection

Rack sensors and PDUs provide shared protection. For TDE, our current DPS scope is PDU control.

26. Measure the full path from fault to confirmed shutdown

We plan to test fault detection through confirmed shutdown, including communications loss, recovery and logging.

27. Collect the inputs, then review the full DPS concept

First collect the connections, then review the full concept. Dates remain to be agreed with the contributors.

28. Prepare the hardware review

Prepare the hardware and test plan for review. The immediate needs are consortium contacts and control-engineering support.

29. Backups

The remaining slides are available for questions.
