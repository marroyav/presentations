VD detector protection — speaker notes
I&I draft, 27 October 2026
Eight main slides; nine backup slides.

Opening

For VD, I propose extending the existing BDE PLC system. We already have the
panel drawings, parts lists, power calculations and Ignition front end. The
work is to add the other VD interfaces, test the complete protection paths,
and have the hardware ready for review and procurement in June 2027.

1. VD detector protection

This talk covers VD. HD can later use an extension of this system or its own
PLC. Marco's introduction supplies the wider system context; this talk explains
the hardware we can reuse and the work needed to cover the rest of VD.

2. BDE already has the PLC and panel design

The existing BDE design gives us a commercial PLC, distributed EtherCAT I/O,
supply inhibits and an Ignition front end. For VD, ten auxiliary rack groups
serve eighty WIECs through twenty PL506 supplies. We have enough material to
start the VD engineering from an actual design.

That is the reason for proposing a commercial PLC: we can reuse the design,
expand it with industrial components and maintain it with controls-engineering
support. No critical sub-millisecond function has been identified so far.
The hardware tests will establish whether each complete path meets its limit.

3. Put the VD PLC in the CUC, with I/O near the equipment

I propose placing the VD controller in the CUC beside the other PLC racks.
The I/O remains distributed near the equipment, using the BDE arrangement
along the mezzanine. The central PLC applies the protection rules shared
between systems; local equipment protection remains at the equipment.

OPC UA connects DPS to SC for monitoring and allowed supervisory operations.
The facility input in this diagram represents selected conditions. Its
hardware or software path depends on the function. The diagram does not
assign every protection input to OPC UA.

The June and October detector layouts still mark the central PLC rack TBD.
CUC placement is our proposal, not a documented rack assignment. The hardware
package includes the CUC-to-VD fieldbus route and control-power allocation.

4. Add the VD interfaces that the BDE system does not cover

The equipment inventories already set the scale. PDS has eighty PoF boxes
and fifty-eight planned low-voltage feeds. TDE occupies twenty rack positions.
CalCI supplies level, temperature and optical-source information, and needs
interfaces for the applicable protection permits. Facility conditions connect
through the separate LBNF/DUNE and SDSTA building systems and cryogenic systems.

PoF is a useful example. Eight temperature and eight voltage readings per box
give 1,280 monitoring values. Those values size the local readout. The permits,
fault indications and shutdown groups determine the DPS connection count.
The proposed extension therefore includes local PoF readout/control and the
protection exchange; it does not allocate 1,280 central PLC terminals.

The existing ICD matrix supplies the requirements that affect these purchases.
One real conflict concerns purity-monitor light and PDS bias: earlier sources
require bias off, while the later working PDS matrix allows acquisition with
activity tagging. The chosen behaviour changes the required permits and
feedback. That belongs in the hardware discussion now; detailed thresholds
and the full response matrix can mature later.

The TDE draft already describes local microTCA crate protection. Its external
DPS inhibit remains a separate interface. The table describes proposed work,
not agreed trip rules or finished terminal quantities. Personnel-safety alarms
remain with SDSTA, as Marco describes in the supplied email.

5. Protection must keep working when SC is unavailable

The proposed division is that SC operates, sequences, displays and records;
DPS and local protection enforce the equipment limits. An operating request
cannot override a protection trip. Reset clears the trip only when permitted;
re-enabling the equipment is a separate action.

The PX3 guide makes the integration work concrete. Ignition normally monitors
sensors and switches outlets over SNMP. When Ignition is unreachable, the PDU
uses local rules for smoke, water and temperature and keeps the network-switch
outlet powered. It does not automatically restore tripped outlets.

The prototype with Geoff and the rack team must exercise this configuration
with PLC protection and SC recovery. Include the case where the host still
answers ping but its protection application has stalled: the guide's ping
check establishes reachability, not application health. This is a proposed
test, not a reported failure in an installed rack.

6. Test the full protection path before buying the additions

Use the BDE vertical-slice work and the G271 equipment to test representative
VD paths. Inject a sensor or equipment fault, observe the PLC and I/O action,
and measure when the affected supply or load reaches its protective state.
The acceptance limit comes from the equipment owner and includes detection
through completed shutdown. A PLC scan-time figure alone is insufficient.

Then exercise SC loss, fieldbus interruption, control-power loss, reset and
restart. The simulation framework prepares load and fault cases; the physical
bench measures PLC, communication and equipment behaviour. A measured path
that cannot meet its allowance needs a faster local action or another interface.

June's SC progress presentation describes existing BDE vertical-slice work.
The July G271 deck lists installed PDU, PL506, mini-MPOD and switch; WIEC/PLC
additions were still planned in that document. These records establish useful
test resources, not a completed VD prototype or current availability booking.

7. Take the VD hardware to review in June 2027

The review package follows directly from the design and tests: VD panel and
I/O drawings, CUC fieldbus routes, power allocation, added parts and cables,
test results, costs, lead times and spares. The existing component and power
records supply the starting quantities and calculation method. New purchases
cover the VD allocation and added interfaces.

The hardware design and procurement-readiness review precede ordering. Site
installation follows the required sequence; some facility interfaces may be
needed in summer 2027. The full VD response matrix is due for review in early
2028. The early matrix work has the narrower job of supplying the requirements
that can change the hardware we buy.

These dates follow Marco's proposed milestones. Intermediate monthly dates
are not assigned without the engineering effort and test time to support them.

10. One BDE group
Ten groups each have two PL506 supplies, eight WIECs and forty-eight WIBs:

wiring for readability. Group, coupler, panel and rack counts are distinct.

11. Panel drawings and Ignition
The two figures are genuine source excerpts: EDMS 2401090 v4 sheet 2 and
June SC Progress PDF page 7. The parts list names CX5140, EK1100, EK1521,
EL2124 and a TF6100 OPC UA licence, among the other field I/O and supplies.
The earlier TPC drawing's panel sizes and older populations do not override
the VD workbook's allocations. Historical prices are not current quotations.

12. Bias-panel parts
The VD workbook specifies two 337 mm DIN rails per rack. Each has an EK1100,
EL9410, EL9011, eighteen EL2124 terminals and a 24 V supply. Ten racks imply
twenty rails and 360 EL2124 terminals, or 1,440 output channels in that draft.
The working VD inventory has 576 bias channels. The inhibit-to-supply mapping
sets the purchase quantity; this difference is not verified spare capacity.

13. Power estimates
BDE LV RACK!D27 records 2,254 W expected AC input per PL506 for four WIECs.
Twenty supplies therefore imply 45.08 kW, shown as about 45.1 kW.
BDE Aux Rack!D14 records 831 W. K13:K14 identify its six-WIEC origin and an
approximately 33% increase for eight. A linear planning estimate is therefore
831 x 8/6 = 1,108 W per auxiliary rack, or 11.08 kW for ten. This is an
explicit scaling assumption, not a fresh terminal-by-terminal calculation.
Bias HV Rack!K22 gives 24 V x 40 A = 960 W supply capacity. Capacity and AC
input demand are different quantities and are not added into a system total.
The older DDSS workbook provides useful calculations, but its 25 LV / five
bias rack population and some terminal types differ from the VD design.

14. PX3 behaviour
The June 2026 guide documents normal Ignition protection and conditional local
fallback. It preserves NET_SWITCH and requires deliberate power restoration.
It does not establish identical installation in every VD rack or a tested PLC
permit interface. PX4/iX12 capabilities are not attributed to this PX3 setup.

15. Other VD equipment
The working PDS allocation uses 42 DAPHNE feeds, eight calibration-board feeds
and eight pulser-bias feeds: 58 total. It proposes seven MPV8060i modules and
one MPV8030i in at least one full-size MPOD crate. These are subsystem supply
allocations, not DPS terminal quantities. PoF has no direct Ethernet interface
in the inventory; its local readout/control needs the proposed interface.
CalCI includes cryogenic instrumentation, purity monitors, ionisation lasers
and the pulsed-neutron source without duplicating their inventory entries.

16. Facility inspection
U-FD-M-901 rev12 (14 February 2025) has twenty control-panel symbols, including
eight CUC references and the north/south 4910 sump interfaces. The maps and
survey register identify drawing references, not twenty confirmed installed
cabinets. Field observations are pending. No inspection or external contact
was performed as part of this presentation revision.

17. Rack inventory
The May workbook has 76 numbered mezzanine positions, SEDM-01 through SEDM-76,
including reserved and spare positions. It separately describes forty top-plate
mini-racks. Neither count establishes the number of remote-I/O stations.
