VD detector protection — speaker notes
I&I draft, 27 October 2026
Twelve main slides; seven backup slides.

Opening

DPS receives information from FFDG BMS, SDSTA BMS, cryogenics and the detector.
It uses those conditions to arbitrate detector actions. Slow controls operates
and monitors the detector and keeps the logs. I will first show those roles
and the detector connections, then the BDE hardware we already have, and then
how we propose extending it to the rest of VD.

1. VD detector protection

The talk covers VD. HD can later use an extension or a separate PLC. The
sequence is system definitions, system inputs, detector detail, existing BDE
engineering, the VD proposal, and the work needed before procurement.

2. DPS arbitrates protection; SC operates, monitors and logs

DPS is the Detector Protection System. It combines the relevant facility and
detector conditions to allow or block detector actions. It issues permits,
inhibits or trips. SC is slow controls: it runs and monitors the detector,
provides the Ignition interface and owns the data and event logs. Those logs
include the conditions, DPS decisions and resulting equipment states.

BMS means Building Management System. FFDG BMS and SDSTA BMS are separate
inputs, each controlling its own building services. A local interlock is
protection at the equipment that can trip without waiting for SC or the
central DPS. An operating command cannot override a protection trip.

These restore the first presentation's definitions and incorporate Manuel's
explicit SC logging and DPS arbitration roles. The older SC–DPS interface
provides background; the current architecture inputs govern this presentation.

3. DPS receives inputs from four system domains

Keep the four sources visible: FFDG BMS, SDSTA BMS, cryogenics and detector.
The detector contains SC and the subsystems. They supply operating requests,
equipment conditions and faults; the building and cryogenic systems supply
the relevant service conditions and alarms. DPS arbitrates the resulting
actions. SC records the relevant inputs, decisions and equipment states.

For example, a low liquid-level condition can withdraw the high-voltage permit.
SC logs the condition, decision and actual HV state. This example does not
assign the liquid-level sensor to a particular system: the selected cryogenic
instrumentation signal and its route belong in the interface design.

The diagram shows logical inputs, not a claim that every connection uses the
same protocol. Facility and cryogenic controls retain their own service
control. SDSTA retains personnel-safety responsibility.

4. Detector: SC talks to the DPS PLC over OPC UA

This expands the detector protection path. SC/Ignition exchanges control
requests, status and events with the DPS PLC over OPC UA. The PLC performs
arbitration and sends the permits or inhibits to the subsystem interfaces.
Conditions and fault indications return from the subsystems to the PLC.
SC receives the resulting decisions and states over OPC UA and owns the logs.

The subsystem block contains BDE, high voltage, PDS, TDE and CalCI. PDS is
photon detection; TDE is top drift electronics; CalCI covers calibration and
cryogenic instrumentation. Local interlocks remain at the equipment. The
next slide expands the existing BDE hardware behind this subsystem interface.

5. BDE already defines a distributed protection system

BDE is bottom drift electronics. The existing VD allocation has ten auxiliary
I/O groups. Each group serves two PL506 low-voltage supplies and eight WIECs.
A WIEC is a warm interface electronics crate; each has a power and timing
card, or PTC, and six warm interface boards, or WIBs. The VD totals are twenty
PL506 supplies, eighty WIECs/PTCs and 480 WIBs.

The diagram shows the supply inhibit, EtherCAT exchange with the PTCs and
48 V power path. Auxiliary I/O also connects the fans, heaters and temperature
sensors. This is an existing distributed design, not a request to discover
whether panels or I/O have been planned.

Sources: June 2026 SC layout and Ignition screens, May VD rack workbook and
the BDE ICD. A rack, physical panel, coupler and software group are distinct
counts; the diagram uses the documented group arrangement.

The VD rack workbook supplies the ten BDE auxiliary and ten bias-HV rack

7. VD proposal: a PLC in the CUC, I/O at the detector

Use a commercial PLC and distributed I/O, extending the BDE design to the
remaining VD systems. Place the central PLC in the CUC beside the other PLC
racks, with I/O near the equipment. A PLC is a programmable logic controller;
CUC is the Central Utility Cavern. This location is proposed: the source
control drawings do not yet assign a DPS rack.

The diagram separates the central PLC from equipment in the detector racks.
SC/Ignition communicates with the PLC over OPC UA and owns the logs. The
additional PDS/TDE/CalCI interfaces connect through SC over OPC UA. Their
exchange with DPS follows the SC–PLC OPC UA connection. The existing BDE/bias
I/O connects to the PLC over EtherCAT. The PDUs are controlled directly by
the DPS PLC and return state and trip feedback to it. The PDU control chain
and local trips are expanded on the next slide.

FFDG BMS, SDSTA BMS and cryogenics supply the facility inputs. The dashed
connection represents the selected hardware or software path for each input.
The design work includes those paths, the CUC fieldbus route and control-power
allocation. The added interfaces use the OPC UA route through SC; EtherCAT
is shown on the existing BDE/bias I/O connection.

Reuse, industrial component support and expansion are the reasons for this
PLC proposal. No critical sub-millisecond protection function has been
identified so far; the prototype must measure the complete required paths.

8. Ignition controls the PLC; the PLC controls the PDU

This is one control chain: Ignition → DPS PLC → PDU → rack equipment.
Ignition supplies the control interface. The PLC arbitrates the requests
against the protection conditions and commands the PDU. A PDU is a power
distribution unit. Local interlocks can still trip on the applicable hazards
without waiting for the central command path.

PDU state and trip feedback return to the PLC. SC records that state and the
DPS decisions and events. An Ignition command cannot override an active trip.
Reset and re-enable remain separate actions. The prototype tests this chain,
the local trips and the status returned to SC.

Ignition and the PLC occupy different stages of this control chain. The PX3
material supplies reference local functions and recovery behaviour for the
prototype.

9. The VD extension adds subsystem and facility interfaces

Use 34 for the PDS LV count, as supplied by Manuel. The older inventory's
per-device totals are not used to reconstruct that count. The table also
uses eighty PoF boxes and twenty TDE rack positions. CalCI and HV need level,
temperature, optical-source status and the applicable permits or inhibits.
The FFDG BMS, SDSTA BMS and cryogenic interfaces provide selected conditions
for DPS arbitration.

PoF means power over fibre. Eight temperature and eight voltage readings per
box give 1,280 monitoring values. That population sizes the local readout.
The protection permits, fault indications and shutdown groups determine the
DPS I/O. Monitoring values are not one-for-one central PLC terminal counts.

The existing ICD matrix supplies the requirements that affect these hardware
connections. For example, purity-monitor light and PDS bias requirements differ
between the earlier source and the later working interaction matrix. The
selected rule changes the needed permits and feedback. This is the part of
matrix work needed before purchasing; detailed thresholds can mature later.

10. Test arbitration, trips and recovery on the bench

Use the existing BDE vertical-slice work and G271 equipment for representative
paths. Exercise conflicting requests, bad conditions and the resulting PLC
arbitration. Measure detection through completed shutdown against the equipment
owner's allowed time. A PLC scan-time figure alone does not establish it.

Test local PDU trips, SC and fieldbus loss, reset and re-enable. Observe actual
equipment state, and verify that SC logs the decisions and resulting states.
The simulation framework prepares load and fault cases. The physical bench
measures PLC, communications and equipment response.

The June BDE material describes vertical-slice work. The July G271 deck lists
installed PDU, PL506, mini-MPOD and switch; WIEC/PLC additions were then planned.
Those sources establish test resources, not a completed VD qualification.

Backup slides

13. VD rack space
Ten BDE auxiliary racks and ten bias-HV racks each have 12U DPS allocations in
the May workbook. These are design allocations, not installed assemblies.

14. Bias-panel parts
The workbook gives two 337 mm rails per bias rack. Each contains one EK1100,
one EL9410, one EL9011, eighteen EL2124 terminals and one 24 V supply. Ten racks
imply twenty rails and 360 EL2124 terminals, or 1,440 output channels in that
first-pass layout. The working VD bias inventory has 576 channels. The actual
inhibit mapping determines the purchase quantity; the difference is not
verified spare capacity.

15. Power
The workbook records 2,254 W expected AC input per PL506. Twenty imply 45.08 kW,
shown as about 45.1 kW. Its 831 W auxiliary estimate has a six-WIEC origin and
a note requiring about 33% more for eight. Explicit linear scaling gives
831 x 8/6 = 1,108 W per rack, or 11.08 kW for ten. This is a planning estimate.
The bias supply allowance is 24 V x 40 A = 960 W DC capacity. It is distinct
from AC demand and is not added into a combined input-power total.

16. Local PDU trips and recovery
The PX3 guide describes local event rules/scripts for smoke, water and
temperature, preservation of the network-switch outlet during its fallback
shutdown, and deliberate power restoration. The VD control chain remains
Ignition → DPS PLC → PDU. Feedback reaches the PLC and SC's logs.

17. Other VD equipment
The PDS LV entry is 34. Retain the PoF monitoring and TDE rack populations and
the CalCI equipment list. No new MPOD module allocation is derived from the
superseded PDS feed calculation. SC handles monitoring and logging; DPS
arbitrates the relevant permits and inhibits.

18. Facility inspection
U-FD-M-901 rev12 has twenty control-panel symbols, including eight CUC
references and the north/south 4910 sump interfaces. These are mapped drawing
references; actual controllers, terminals and I/O await the field inspection.

19. Rack inventory
