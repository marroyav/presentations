Building the detector protection system — speaker notes
Draft revised 27 October 2026

Purpose and provenance
This revision uses the email thread supplied by Manuel: his 23 October draft
request, Marco's 25 October response, Vivian's 26 October response, and Manuel's
undated reply. It does not claim fresh EDMS verification or new hardware tests.
The revised talk complements Marco's proposed introduction to system roles.
The earlier deck and reference records are preserved in archive/.

Slide 2 — Proposed PLC direction
Manuel intends to use a commercial PLC based on BDE, with distributed I/O and
OPC UA for monitoring/orchestration. Marco and Vivian favour an industrial PLC.
This is a proposed design direction, not an approved purchase or vendor choice.
Explain why it suits maintenance and expansion. Do not claim a guaranteed PLC
response time or that FPGA hardware is ruled out for every local function.

Slide 3 — What exists
Manuel confirms that the BDE PLC design documents, slides and Ignition front
end are already available. With Terri and Trevor, identify which parts can be
reused and what the wider DPS needs to add. Check capacity and performance for
the expanded system; availability of the material does not qualify that expansion.
Use the documented BDE panel layout and I/O capacity to size and place the
additional DPS I/O. Manuel confirms this information should already be in the
available BDE material. The working inventory records EtherCAT protection links
and distributed panels; it does not qualify the expanded DPS.
The older shared infrastructure inventory assumes SNMP for the PLC supervisory
interface. This assumption needs reconciliation with the proposed OPC UA path.
Do not carry draft module or endpoint totals into procurement as agreed counts.

Slide 4 — Proposed topology
The diagram shows function and placement, not final wiring. Decide one central
PLC versus multiple PLCs, failure domains, fieldbus segments and network access.
Equipment status may include direct sensors or local controller feedback.
Determine which information must use dedicated I/O rather than a software link.
OPC UA monitoring or commands must not imply permission to override protection.

Slide 5 — Rack protection
PDU means power distribution unit. A local interlock is a protection action
implemented at the equipment. Confirm each PDU model's sensor support, outlet
switching, external permit and trip contacts, network behaviour and reset.
Do not assume sensor support includes automatic shutdown or a hardwired permit.

Slide 6 — Minimal inventory and ICDs
The existing matrix collects consortium requests and identifies missing paths
and conflicting requirements. Use only the detail needed for hardware sizing
and ICD updates now; the full response matrix can develop in parallel.
For each path record the source, destination, physical location, electrical
signal/range, channel count, protocol, total response requirement and failure
behaviour. Track each unresolved item with a contact and an agreed due date.
Remove duplicate material and add CALCI; do not invent its scope or expansion.

Slide 7 — Timing
Manuel has not yet identified a critical function requiring sub-ms response.
This is a review finding, not proof that no such requirement exists. Ask the
consortia for the allowable time from fault detection to completed shutdown.
Include sensor response, transport, PLC scan/load, outputs and actuator delay.
A simulation can prepare and explore cases; hardware tests establish performance
for the actual tested configuration. Confirm timing before procurement.

Slide 8 — Boundaries
Marco distinguishes LBNF/DUNE BMS and SDSTA BMS; hardware I/O is expected to be
limited and its termination point remains open. Software exchange is proposed
for monitoring. SDSTA retains personnel-safety alarm responsibility.
Do not assume ODH/fire/smoke signals belong to LBNF/DUNE BMS. Do not assume a
liquid-level value originates in cryogenic controls: it may come from cryogenic
instrumentation through SC. Confirm source, owner, quality and failure state.
Work with Geoff on SC/DPS command arbitration, reset and re-enable. Relevant
long-term data should be logged through SC; avoid independent duplicate archives.

Slide 9 — Survey
Manuel reports marked inspection maps for the 4850 and 4910 levels; these have
not yet been shared. Underground and Ross Dry inspection is planned this week,
subject to access and safety arrangements being coordinated with Rachel.
Dale Curran is a possible SDSTA integration contact identified by Manuel, who
reports that Dale recently supported fire-system integration. Contact details
are not reproduced and no meeting or commitment is claimed.
Survey what is installed and where to connect. SDSTA input defines interface
facts; it does not transfer responsibility for designing the DUNE DPS to SDSTA.

Slide 10 — Prototype proposal
Agree which equipment, engineering support and hosting are available at
Fermilab, and whether a SURF bench or site test is useful. Test a representative
complete protection path, then PDU behaviour and SC integration. Record required
and measured timing, tested configuration, faults and recovery. Include loss of
signal, fieldbus, network and power. No bench or test results are claimed here.

Slide 11 — Schedule and procurement
June 2027 design review, possible summer 2027 building interfaces and early 2028
VD response-matrix review come from Marco's proposed path. The intervening
work periods are planning proposals added in this revision, not agreed dates.
Prepare a bill of materials, spare capacity, cable/panel specifications, costs,
lead times and deployment sequence. Review procurement readiness before launch.
Check long-lead risks and early site needs before promising delivery dates.

Slide 12 — Responsibilities
Manuel offers to lead hardware work with a process control engineer. Trevor is
suggested as possible engineering support, not assigned to that role. Confirm
support, reviewers, procurement owners and dates. Vivian offers to help update
the DPS ICD with Terri, Trevor and others. Proposed contacts are not commitments.
For installation, integration, commissioning and operation, agree who protects
equipment while DPS is absent. Manuel proposes responsibility remains with the
consortium owning that equipment; no agreement is claimed.
