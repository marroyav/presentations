Presentation architecture supplied by Manuel — 27 October 2026

These instructions take precedence over older working inventories and earlier
assistant revisions of the presentation.

- Restore the definitions from the original presentation.
- Show four DPS input domains separately: FFDG BMS, SDSTA BMS, cryogenics,
  and detector. Within detector, show SC and the detector subsystems.
- SC operates and monitors the detector and owns the logs.
- DPS performs arbitration: it uses the relevant conditions to permit or
  inhibit detector actions. SC cannot override a protection trip.
- Slide 4: DPS controls both PDUs and LV power supplies using the same
  device protocol: SNMP. Group them on one control/status connection from
  the DPS PLC. The previous assistant drawing incorrectly separated their
  control paths and treated the BDE supply-inhibit wiring as the LV protocol.
  The subsystem OPC UA path goes through SC, and SC communicates with the
  DPS PLC over OPC UA. Replace the generic direct PLC–subsystem arrows.
  Keep the dedicated BDE I/O description on the following slide.
- SC–OPC UA bridge servers are outside this presentation. Do not add them
  to the diagrams or describe their implementation in the talk.
- Expand the detector section, then show the existing BDE design and its
  definitions before introducing the proposed VD extension.
- PDS LV count is 34. This replaces the previous LV-feed count in the slides.
  Do not reconstruct the count from the older PDS inventory's device totals.
- Control chain: Ignition controls the PLC; the PLC controls the PDU.
  The PLC performs arbitration. Local interlocks remain able to trip.
  This is one control chain, not competing control owners or a transfer
  from Ignition to the PLC.
- Slide 7: PDUs and LV supplies share the direct SNMP control/status
  connection to the DPS PLC. The
  additional PDS/TDE/CalCI interfaces use OPC UA through SC, which communicates
  with the DPS PLC over OPC UA. Do not draw those additional interfaces as
  a direct PLC branch. Existing BDE/bias I/O uses EtherCAT to the PLC.
- TDE: current control is through its PDUs only. Retain TDE in the proposal
  diagram and keep crate status / external inhibit as possible future
  interfaces if needed. Do not describe them as available capabilities.
- Keep VD as the current scope. HD can later extend this system or use
  another PLC. CUC placement beside the other PLC racks is proposed.

Protocol evidence for the shared device connection
The PX3 Rack Protection Setup guide, DUNE-doc-32655 v8, sections 2 and 4.3,
specifies SNMP. The BDE ICD, EDMS 3293079 v1, section 3.2 (PDF p6), specifies
SNMP agents for WIENER PL506 and MPOD supplies. Ting Miao's SC Progress,
10 June 2026, slide 26 (PDF p27), also groups WIENER/ISEG supplies and PDUs
under SNMP. These sources establish the device protocol; Manuel's
instructions place the device-control connection at the DPS PLC in this
presentation. The BDE ICD's TTL inhibits (section 5, PDF p7) remain additional
hardware protection interfaces and are described on the BDE detail slide.
