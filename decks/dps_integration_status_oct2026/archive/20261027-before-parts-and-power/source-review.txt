DPS I&I presentation — review of the available material
27 October 2026 | Scope: vertical drift (VD)

The presentation should explain how we extend the documented BDE hardware to
cover VD. We already have rack allocations, panel designs, an equipment list,
power calculations, a control architecture, Ignition screens, test-stand
material and a first set of subsystem requirements. The engineering task is
to reconcile these records and specify the additions.

Earlier edits treated several documented items as information still to be
collected. That understated the work already done and made the proposed next
steps too vague. This review corrects that reading of the source material.

1. What the rack records establish

FD2_Rack_Estimates_May_2025_TMS.xlsx, RACKS sheet:
- 20 BDE LV racks, 10 BDE auxiliary racks, 10 bias-HV racks.
- 20 TDE, 4 PD LV, 2 drift-HV, 2 reserved drift-HV, 8 spare/CalCI positions.
- Total: 76 numbered mezzanine positions, SEDM-01 through SEDM-76.
- A separate sheet describes 40 top-plate mini-racks.

BDE Aux Rack!B14:C14 reserves 12U for DPS equipment. B57 and B59:B61 list
8 dual-fibre PTC connections, 8 fan cables, 8 heater cables and 8 RTD cables.
Bias HV Rack!B15:C15 also reserves 12U for the DPS interface to the ISEG supplies.
Its B3 note explicitly calls this a first pass based on the earlier detector
layout. The workbook supplies a real space and cabling basis, with that limit.

Thus the VD allocation has ten BDE auxiliary locations and ten bias-HV
locations with 12U DPS allowances. These are rack-space allocations, not a
claim that twenty completed panel assemblies exist. An I/O station, a physical
panel, a rack position and a software group are different counts.

The shared infrastructure draft lists 'DPS remote I/O: 76'. The reviewed
primary records establish 76 rack positions, not 76 remote-I/O stations. Do not
use that number as an I/O quantity without its separate source and mapping.
June's architecture shows ten BDE groups and ten bias-HV groups. The supplied
October architecture has additional remote-I/O multiplicity markings; use its
panel/coupler map to count stations rather than equating them to rack totals.

The May allocation and later March/July 80-rack planning statements need a
category/revision check before ordering. A different total does not erase the
known BDE rack allocation or require starting the inventory again.

2. The VD BDE grouping is explicit

Ting Miao, Slow Controls Progress, 10 June 2026, slide 6 (PDF page 7):
20 rows of four WIECs and one PL506 each, grouped into DPS PLC I/O-01 through
I/O-10. It includes an actual BDE Ignition front-end screenshot.

Ting Miao, SC Infrastructure and Installation, same date, slide 4 (PDF page 5):
one BDE auxiliary rack per group; two LV racks per group; four WIECs per PL506.
The diagram shows EtherCAT to the PTCs, power to fans/heaters and supply
interlocks. It separately shows ten bias-HV rack groups and drift-HV I/O.

EDMS 3293079 v1, attachment internal revision 1.4, pp. 4–7:
80 WIECs for VD, six WIBs per WIEC, 20 PL506 supplies. Section 5 specifies
PL506 channel inhibits, bias-supply inhibits, fan/heater status and a
bidirectional PLC/PTC path. Fan/heater inhibit grouping is still a decision
in that text. Monitoring sampling rates are not a qualified trip time.

The slides can therefore show 10 BDE groups -> 20 PL506 supplies -> 80 WIECs
and 480 WIBs. Use the documented architecture and screen; describe what must
be added for the other VD systems.

3. Panel drawings, components and power calculations already exist

EDMS 2401090 v4, CE Safety Interlock System, contains:

  includes central-PLC, bias-I/O and LV/fan/heater panel arrangements.
- CE_Interlock_Parts_List.xlsx, updated 11 April 2023: CX5140 PLC, EK1100
  couplers, OPC UA licensing, 5 V outputs, RTD modules, fan inputs/outputs,
  fibre interfaces, supplies, wiring, production spares and historical costs.
- DDSS_Controls_Power_Analysis.xlsx: PLC, TPC-LV and bias-HV rack calculations.
- Interface_to_DUNE_Detector_Safety_System: architecture and panel explanation.

These are useful engineering starting documents. Their older 25-LV-rack,
150-flange and five-bias-rack quantities are not the VD quantities. The older
drawing uses a CX5120; the 2023 list uses a CX5140. That is a specific revision
reconciliation, not a reason to say the PLC design is unknown. Historical costs
need updating before procurement; production spares do not establish unused
installed I/O channels.

4. Rack protection has a concrete design question

Brandon Howe, PX3 Rack Protection Setup, 5 June 2026, DUNE-doc-32655 v8,
pp. 3–6 and 14–16:
- Ignition normally reads sensors and commands outlets over SNMP.
- When Ignition is unreachable, PX3 event rules and Lua provide local fallback.
- The fallback handles smoke, water and temperature, preserving NET_SWITCH.
- Power is not automatically restored after a fallback trip.
- The reference configuration uses a fixed 60 C temperature fallback and
  5000 ms SNMP polling. These are configuration values, not universal VD limits.
- Ignition reachability is checked by ping. A responsive host does not establish
  that its protection application is processing sensor data and commands.

The original deck asked whether the PDU could do things already described in
this guide. The useful work is to agree whether normal rack protection remains
in Ignition, becomes local in the PDU, or is implemented in DPS, and how each
system exchanges trip status, requests and reset. This is directly relevant to
Manuel's proposed boundary and to the hardware connection count.

A targeted prototype test should leave the gateway host reachable while the
relevant application/driver is stalled. Also test network loss, rule behaviour,
trip feedback and deliberate recovery. This is a proposed test, not an observed
failure of an installed rack.

The later PX4/iX12 notes describe different models and candidate interfaces.
Do not transfer those capabilities or accessory assumptions to the PX3 setup
without the selected model/firmware and circuit definition.

5. The matrix has a bounded job before procurement

The existing integration matrix has 46 rules from 16 sources. It already
records omissions, inferences and conflicts. Reuse it to extract the signals
that affect hardware: source and destination, electrical interface, protocol,
quantity, allowable total response, loss-of-signal state and feedback.

A useful example is the conflict over PrM illumination and PDS bias: the
existing sources disagree about bias exclusion versus continued acquisition
with activity tagging. That can change which physical permits or status
connections are needed. The discussion therefore belongs before purchasing,
even though detailed thresholds and the full response matrix can mature later.

The PDS inventory supplies another concrete example: 80 PoF boxes with 8
voltage and 8 temperature readings per box imply 1,280 monitoring values.
They do not imply 1,280 central-PLC terminals. The local acquisition, signal
conditioning, aggregation and protective output decide the module count.
Group calibration and cryogenic instrumentation under CalCI without duplicating
items already present in the subsystem lists. Verify which CalCI interfaces
are required rather than adding a second generic hardware inventory.

6. The PLC proposal and the CUC location

A commercial PLC with distributed I/O is supported by the existing BDE design,
Manuel's stated intent and the supplied Marco/Vivian discussion. It should be
presented as the proposed VD implementation. The choice rests on reuse,
maintainability, I/O compatibility and measured behaviour for the required
functions. Absence of a known sub-ms function alone is not a timing qualification.

Use a complete timing budget: hazardous condition, sensor detection, transport,
PLC processing, output and completed equipment action. The current SC emulator
results concern software monitoring/configuration workloads on a host; they do
not measure the PLC/fieldbus/actuator trip path. Use the framework to prepare
requirements and fault cases, then measure representative hardware paths.

The central PLC can be proposed in the CUC with distributed I/O at detector
racks. The June and supplied October detector-control diagrams still label
its rack TBD. The process-controls architecture shows CUC cryogenic/ODH PLCs,
but does not assign the DPS a rack beside them. Specify the CUC-to-VD fieldbus
route and control-power/network dependencies as part of that placement.

Per Manuel's latest instruction, this presentation covers VD. HD will later
use an extension or a separate PLC. Do not include HD quantities in the VD
parts list or turn that future choice into an immediate VD requirement.

7. What the workshop should achieve

Show the VD hardware and quantities first, then identify the additions and
specific unresolved interfaces. Marco's system introduction can carry the
broader system-role explanation. Manuel's talk should establish:
- the BDE hardware and software to reuse;
- a VD rack/panel/I/O allocation for the added subsystem connections;
- the SC/DPS/PDU control decision, timing requirements and facility exchanges;
- prototype work using the existing Fermilab arrangements;
- the drawings, parts list, costs, lead times, spares, results and installation
  sequence needed for the June 2027 design and procurement-readiness review.

The July G271 deck (DUNE-doc-37378 v2, pp. 3–4) distinguishes installed PDU,
PL506, mini-MPOD and switch from then-planned WIEC/PLC additions. The June SC
progress deck also identifies the IERC vertical-slice work. Use this equipment
and experience, while checking current availability and the configuration needed
for each added test. No new bench results were produced by this review.

The supplied email proposes possible summer 2027 facility interfaces and the
full VD response-matrix review in early 2028. There is no need to invent exact
monthly milestones before the engineering support and test time are agreed.
