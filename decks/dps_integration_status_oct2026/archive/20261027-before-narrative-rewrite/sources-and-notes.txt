DPS hardware for vertical drift — speaker notes
Reviewed draft, 27 October 2026

The main argument
Use the existing BDE PLC design for VD. Show its racks, panels, distributed I/O
and software first. Then explain the extra interfaces, prototype work and
purchases needed. HD is a later extension or separate PLC, as Manuel requested.

1. Title
Fifteen main slides including the title, followed by one inventory reference.
This talk complements Marco's proposed explanation of the different systems.

2. Use a commercial PLC
The proposal follows Manuel's intent and Marco/Vivian's email discussion.
BDE already provides a commercial PLC design, parts list and distributed I/O.
Propose the central PLC in the CUC; the source detector layouts still say
TBD rack. Do not imply that a DPS rack space beside cryogenic PLCs is assigned.
OPC UA is the intended supervisory interface; agree which commands are allowed.
The older hardware parts list includes an OPC UA licence, and the supplied
October detector architecture labels the PLC interface OPC UA and ADS.

3. VD rack space
The May workbook lists 20 BDE LV racks, 10 auxiliary racks and 10 bias-HV racks.
It reserves 12U in each auxiliary rack and each bias-HV rack for DPS hardware.
The BDE auxiliary sheet lists eight WIEC/PTC fibre links, fan, heater and RTD
connections per rack. The bias-HV sheet says its layout is a first estimate.
Describe twenty rack locations with DPS allocations, not twenty proven panel
assemblies. The workbook's U reservations do not state unused channel capacity.

4. BDE grouping
The June infrastructure drawing repeats ten BDE groups, each with one auxiliary
rack, two LV racks/PL506s and eight WIECs. The June Ignition layout shows I/O-01
through I/O-10 and 20 rows, each with one PL506 and four WIECs. The BDE ICD gives
six WIBs per VD WIEC. The totals are 20 PL506s, 80 PTCs/WIECs and 480 WIBs.
The simplified diagram shows the PL506 inhibits, PTC EtherCAT link and LV feed.
Fan/heater/RTD connections are mentioned below it rather than drawn.
An I/O group can contain several couplers or physical panels; do not equate
these numbers to a terminal/module count.

6. VD bias-panel components
The May VD workbook's Bias HV Rack!K17:K21 specifies two identical DIN rails
per rack, each with 1 EK1100, 1 EL9011, 1 EL9410, 18 EL2124 and one 24 V power
supply, occupying 337 mm. Ten racks therefore imply 20 rails, 20 couplers,
20 supply terminals, 20 end caps, 20 supplies and 360 EL2124 modules.
Four outputs per EL2124 imply 1,440 output channels in this first-pass layout.
The working VD bias inventory has 576 channels (480 CRP + 96 field-cage).
This comparison identifies a specific sizing question. Map the chosen ISEG
module inhibits before carrying all 360 output modules into procurement.
Do not describe the difference as verified spare capacity in built panels.

7. VD power values
BDE LV RACK!D27 stores 2,254 W expected AC input for a PL506 supplying four
WIECs; K27:K29 use 48 V, about 8.8 A per WIEC and 75% efficiency. This is
an input-load estimate, not the DPS controller load.
BDE Aux Rack!D14 stores 831 W expected and F14 960 W maximum. K13:K14 say
the calculation originated with six WIECs and needs about 33% more for eight
in VD. K17:K18 also contain earlier 553.68/665 W and efficiency notes. These
are mixed stages of the estimate; do not multiply the displayed 831 W by ten
and call the result the complete VD DPS budget.
Bias HV Rack!D15 and F15 both store 960 W; K22 gives 24 V x 40 A. That is a
supply-capacity allowance and does not independently calculate the bias-I/O
load. Recompute with the VD terminal population and actual field loads.
The older DDSS power workbook records 1.05 A for its PLC rack, 23.06575 A for
its six-flange LV panel and 2.66 A for its bias panel. These are historical
worksheet totals and are not substituted for VD loads. Its bias design uses
EL2088 terminals, unlike the EL2124 layout in the VD rack sheet. The old
worksheet adds E-bus and field-current columns; it does not by itself supply
a verified AC-input calculation for the new panel configuration.

8. PX3 rack protection
The June setup guide already documents normal protection in Ignition and
local PDU fallback when Ignition is unreachable. The fallback preserves the
NET_SWITCH outlet and does not restore power automatically. The diagram of
a local trip must reflect this conditional behaviour rather than implying
that every configured hazard always acts locally.
Agree the normal protection roles with Geoff and the DPS/rack owners. This
is an engineering decision that can change interfaces and required I/O.
No claim is made that all VD racks use that configuration or have passed it.

9. Inventory and hardware additions
Use the existing subsystem lists and the 46-rule matrix. Extract the entries
needed for the VD I/O schedule and bill of materials. Resolve real conflicts,
including PrM/PDS requirements where the assumed protective action changes
which permits or feedback are needed. Keep detailed rule tuning in the later
response-matrix work.
The 80 PoF boxes and 8 T + 8 V per box are Manuel's working inventory: 1,280
monitoring values. Module quantities depend on acquisition, conditioning and
aggregation. CalCI includes calibration and cryogenic instrumentation; merge
overlapping records rather than inventing duplicate equipment.

10. Response time
No critical sub-ms requirement has been identified by Manuel so far. This is
not proof that no faster local function is needed. Ask for the allowable time
from the hazardous condition through detection to completed protective action.
The emulator has software scaling and interface results; it has not qualified
a PLC, EtherCAT chain, output or equipment shutdown. Use it to prepare cases,
then measure the complete paths under representative load and failures.

11. Interfaces
Discuss concrete SC/DPS commands, reset and re-enable with Geoff. Alarm
acknowledgement is separate from reset and permission to energise equipment.
Identify the actual level/pressure source; cryogenic instrumentation and
cryogenic controls are different systems. Choose the required transport rather
than assuming all protection arrives through OPC UA.
Keep LBNF/DUNE BMS and SDSTA BMS separate. Agree the limited hardware exchanges,
termination point, software monitoring and relevant SC history. Personnel-safety
alarms remain SDSTA's responsibility, as described in Marco's email.
CUC placement requires an explicit fieldbus route and power/network allocation.

Manuel is coordinating with Rachel and has identified Dale Curran as an SDSTA

13. Tests
Use the existing Fermilab BDE/VST and G271 work. The July G271 deck records
installed PDU, PL506, mini-MPOD and switch, and then-planned WIEC/PLC additions.
Confirm present equipment and reserve the configuration needed for each test.
Measure one complete PTC/PLC/inhibit path, the PDU protection logic and recovery,
SC/DPS command priority, and branch/power failures.
Include an application-stall case with the gateway host still responding to
ping. The guide's reachability check alone does not establish application health.
This is a proposed test; it is not evidence of a known deployed failure.

14. Review and procurement
Prepare one reconciled VD rack/panel/I/O schedule, additional hardware drawings,
power budget, parts/cable list, test evidence, costs, supplier lead times,
spares and installation sequence. Review readiness before ordering.
June 2027, possible summer 2027 facility interfaces and early 2028 full VD
response-matrix review follow Marco's proposed milestones. Intermediate month
assignments were removed because effort and engineering support are not agreed.
