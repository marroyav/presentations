VD detector protection — speaker notes
I&I workshop draft, 27 October 2026

The argument

We have a substantial BDE and HV hardware starting point. The next step is to
trace each VD inhibit through that equipment, then identify the additions.
Rack infrastructure is a separate system. This turns the matrix into the input
for panel space, power, cabling, prototype tests and procurement before June 2027.
The first path reviews are underway; this talk does not claim a complete new
I/O count or bill of materials.

1. VD detector protection

This talk covers the VD detector. We propose a commercial PLC based on the
existing BDE design, with distributed I/O. HD can later extend the system or
use a separate PLC. The central PLC in the CUC is a proposed placement.

2. DPS arbitrates protection; SC operates, monitors and logs

Keep the original definitions. DPS uses facility and detector conditions to
permit or inhibit actions and protect equipment. SC, using Ignition, operates
and monitors the detector and owns the data and event logs. FFDG BMS and SDSTA
BMS are separate building systems. Equipment can also act through local
interlocks. An operating request cannot override a protection trip.

3. DPS receives inputs from four system domains

The four domains remain FFDG BMS, SDSTA BMS, cryogenics and detector. The
FFDG path is the API through the Batavia server at FNAL, then SC and DPS.
SDSTA is under investigation. Cryogenic process controls provide selected
conditions, but some measurements, including relevant level measurements,
may originate in detector instrumentation and reach DPS through SC.

The detector contains SC, its subsystems and the separate rack-infrastructure
system. Facility and cryogenic controllers keep control of their own services.
SDSTA retains personnel-safety responsibility. The liquid-level/HV example
explains arbitration without assigning a sensor to the wrong controller.

4. VD uses SNMP control and dedicated protection I/O

This is the single overview of the proposed detector connections. SC talks to
the DPS PLC over OPC UA. Additional subsystem exchanges also use OPC UA
through SC. PDU and WIENER-supply control/status use the same SNMP protocol.
They have separate inventories: PDUs belong to rack infrastructure, and the
LV/bias supplies remain subsystem equipment.

BDE and bias protection also have an EtherCAT route to remote I/O and wired
inhibits to the PL506/ISEG supplies. A supply can have both an SNMP control/
status interface and a wired protection input. The diagram shows both roles.
The wired arrow names PL506/ISEG; it does not assign that interface to all PDS
supplies. The Heinzinger control system is described separately in the HVS
material and is not being labelled as an SNMP supply.

SC logs the decisions and states. Bridge-server implementation is outside
this presentation. The PLC's CUC location is proposed, not an allocated rack.
TDE currently has PDU control only; retain its other interfaces as possible
extensions, not available control capabilities.

5. BDE already defines a distributed protection system

The VD baseline is ten groups of eight WIECs. Each group has two PL506 supplies
in two LV racks, an auxiliary I/O rack and eight PTC/WIEC endpoints. Across VD
this is 20 PL506 supplies, 80 WIECs and 480 WIBs. The auxiliary I/O connects
fans, heaters, temperature sensors, PTC EtherCAT and the supply inhibits.

The June 2026 layout and the VD rack workbook supply this grouping. Eight
WIECs is already the VD starting point; do not count an HD six-to-eight
conversion as new VD scope.

6. BDE drawings, parts and software are already available

Show the actual panel drawing and Ignition screen. We have the parts and
power workbooks as well. The panel picture is a historical TPC reference;
it identifies the engineering approach and component families. It does not
prove the final VD population or free terminal space. The June Ignition
screen shows the existing BDE work and the VD grouping.

7. Trace each inhibit to the hardware it uses

Read one matrix condition and action at a time. If it affects several loads,
trace each output branch. Identify the source, input connection, decision,
actuator, power path and feedback. Use the rack and interface documents to
name the equipment, connector, protocol and channel. Include the time from
condition detection to completed action.

The result for each rule is existing equipment it uses, components/cables to
add with quantities, and any choice that changes those quantities. A missing
connection description does not itself prove missing hardware. A network
point does not automatically require a new analog or digital PLC terminal.
Several rules may use the same physical input or output: count it once.

This is how the minimal matrix supports hardware procurement. It does not
require the full operating response matrix to be frozen now.

8. BDE cooling already has an input-to-inhibit path

DPS-C001 proposes inhibiting the affected WIEC supply when cooling is
inadequate. Fan rotation signals pass through the DB25 fan connector and
cable to EL1004 inputs. The distributed I/O communicates with the DPS PLC
over EtherCAT. The PLC uses an EL2124 5 V output to drive the PL506's wired
channel inhibit; the affected 48 V feed supplies the WIEC/PTC/WIB chain.

The ICEBERG design documents EL1004 pull-up inputs, EL2034 fan power and
short/open-circuit diagnostics, EL2124 supply inhibits and the 5 V supply
for that output bank. The fan command and diagnostics are part of deciding
whether a fan is operating. Fans use the auxiliary supply; the cooling row
does not require a whole-PDU shutdown.

The recovered WIEC safety note describes six fans and six rotation-status
signals per WIEC. This implies 48 inputs per eight-WIEC VD group if that
interface is used throughout. The ICEBERG reference describes four fans.
Match the six-fan interface to the populated VD terminal and cable allocation
before counting additions. This is a baseline coverage check, not a claim
that 480 new inputs or any number of new modules must be purchased.

The WIEC note is internal revision 0, modified 19 September 2025, recovered
from DocDB 34374 v1; it is not established here as the released EDMS v2 copy.
The existing hardware families are documented, but the full VD cooling rule,
fan voting, startup treatment and allowed action time are not approved here.

PL506 channel state, interlock state and voltage/current provide the feedback
candidate while its AC feed is retained. The PTC may stop reporting when its
48 V feed is inhibited; its disappearance alone is not proof of shutdown.
The proposed PLC/SC interface carries the result to the SC logs over OPC UA.

9. Rack infrastructure is a separate protection system

Put the rack, AC feeds, PDU(s), smoke and humidity sensors, sensor hub, power,
relays and cables in one inventory. Include temperature and leak sensors
where fitted. Subsystems reference the PDU outlets that power their equipment;
their supplies and dedicated inhibits remain in their subsystem descriptions.

The proposed chain is Ignition/SC to DPS over OPC UA, then DPS to PDU over
SNMP. The PDU reads its sensors and retains local protection. SC logs sensor,
outlet and trip states through DPS. RACK-01 is the smoke-shutdown rule.
Humidity is in the monitoring scope; no humidity shutdown is inferred merely
from the presence of the sensor.

DocDB 32655 v8 supplies a concrete reference package: PX3, DX-PD2C5 powered
contact hub, 4WTAR-B smoke detector and EOLR-1 relay. Its guide describes an
Ignition-direct prototype with local fallback on loss of Ignition. The VD
PLC command path needs corresponding trip, reachability and recovery
configuration; we are not claiming the reference already implements that path.

10. Detector additions follow the existing VD hardware

BDE and HVS are documented starting points. The HVS ICD describes one
Heinzinger PNC HP300000 with cRIO-9049/NI-9239/NI-9269, ten bias crates and
576 bias channels: 480 CRP and 96 FC. The work is to match each required
function to the appropriate existing connection and identify what is absent.

PDS LV count is 34. Do not reconstruct it from the older 58-feed inventory.
For the 80 PoF boxes, carry Opta PLC and Beckhoff PLC alternatives: the June
SC presentation explicitly names both. Each option must cover the same box
signals, local actions and reporting. The corrected working inventory has
eight temperature and eight voltage readings per box, or 1,280 values.
That does not establish a controller-per-box allocation or central DPS I/O
count. Count each option's actual local I/O, power, enclosure and cabling.

TDE has twenty racks, with current control through its PDUs. Preserve crate
status and external inhibit as future options. CalCI already has useful
material for purity monitors, thermometry, level readout and calibration;
trace its status and optical permits from those descriptions. Its equipment
inventory is incomplete, not empty.

11. Facility interfaces need named signals and routes

Use the FFDG API path through Batavia and SC. Each function needs a named
point with a meaning and an update/loss behavior. A separate physical DPS
input is an addition only if that function requires it. The SDSTA survey
must identify what can be exported and where we can connect. Cryogenics
and detector instrumentation must be distinguished by actual signal source.

FAC-01 is a useful example. The released SC/DDSS requirement covers all HVS
supplies on a fire alarm. The HVS draft explicitly includes CRP and FC bias.
The output branches use the documented drift-HV and bias interfaces. First
map the required alarm point and its zone to those loads; the known API route
does not prove that this particular signal is published. The matrix's Top CRP
column also does not by itself represent all bottom and top CRP bias.

12. Test arbitration, trips and recovery on the bench

Use the BDE vertical-slice work and G271 test resources for representative
paths. Inject the relevant fault, observe the output and equipment state,
and measure the full detection-to-action time. Test local trips, SC and bus
loss, power failure, reset and re-enable. Verify the SC event record.

No critical sub-ms protection function has been identified in Manuel's review
so far; consortia must check that assumption. Simulation prepares load and
fault cases. Hardware tests establish the actual response. The June BDE and
July G271 records document available work/resources, not complete VD acceptance.

13. Take the VD hardware to review in June 2027

The path review produces the reused hardware, added channels/cables, quantities
and alternatives. The prototype results and the component allocations then
support panel space, power, drawings, costs, lead times and spares. Procurement
follows hardware/procurement-readiness review. Some facility interfaces may
be needed in summer 2027. Full VD response-matrix review remains early 2028.
These are the proposed milestones in Marco's email.

Backup slides

15. VD rack space
Ten auxiliary and ten bias racks each have 12U DPS allocations. The workbook
marks 19U as Space in each auxiliary rack and 10U in each bias rack. Auxiliary
U9 also has a possible ground-plane readout note. These are planning positions,
not new DPS reservations or evidence of free DIN rail after VD population.

16. Bias-panel parts
Two rails per rack, each with 18 EL2124 terminals, give 360 terminals/1,440
outputs in the first-pass layout across ten racks. The 576 bias channels do
not establish unused capacity without the actual inhibit and interface mapping.

17. Power
The workbook's expected PL506 input is 2,254 W each, or 45.08 kW across twenty.
The six-fan note gives 0.33 A per fan at 24 V. Eight WIECs with six fans each
therefore have 380.16 W of rated fan load alone. This excludes heaters and I/O.
The inherited 831 W auxiliary estimate is not a full VD component budget.
The earlier 8/6 linear extrapolation is no longer presented as that budget.
The bias supply allowance remains 24 V x 40 A = 960 W DC capacity, a different
quantity from expected AC consumption.

18. Rack hardware reference
The rack package includes the PX3, powered hub, smoke detector, relay and
sensor wiring. Humidity-probe identity and allocation must appear in the rack
inventory. The May workbook has a 76-entry rack-protection/PDU allowance;
this is existing planning scope, not 76 newly required devices. Other rack
locations, including the top-plate concept, have their own source allocations.

19. Local PDU trips and recovery
The PX3 guide documents event/script functions for smoke, water and temperature,
a retained network-switch outlet and deliberate restoration. In that guide
local fallback depends on loss of Ignition. Carry its hardware and functions
into the VD design while testing the selected PLC command path and failure
behavior. Do not equate the guide with an installed VD protection function.

20. Other subsystem inventory
Retain the 34 PDS LV entry, 80 PoF boxes, twenty TDE racks and existing CalCI
material. PoF alternatives are Opta PLC and Beckhoff PLC. TDE's extra status/
inhibit interfaces are possible extensions. OPC UA subsystem exchange goes
through SC; bridge-server deployment remains outside the talk.

21. BMS inspection
The twenty symbols on the marked drawings are panel references, not confirmed
installed controllers. Eight are in the CUC area; SP-1 and SP-2 are the two
4910 sump interfaces. Keep the drawing identity and survey results distinct.

22. Rack inventory
The May sheet has 76 numbered mezzanine positions, including reservations,
and a separate concept for forty top-plate mini-racks. Rack positions, PDU
quantities and remote-I/O stations are different counts. The June SC material
also states a concept of remote I/O in 76 racks; that concept is not a populated
terminal schedule and must be compared with the actual inhibit paths.
