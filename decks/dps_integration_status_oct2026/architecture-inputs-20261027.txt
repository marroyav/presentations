Roles and connections
DPS arbitrates protection. SC/Ignition operates, monitors, coordinates requests
and owns the logs. DAQ consumes detector availability and activity from SC.
Equipment owners define protection requirements and allowed action times.
Local interlocks remain effective; an operating request cannot override a trip.

FFDG DUNE FD BMS -> SC; SDSTA BMS -> SC; cryogenics -> SC.
SC <-> OPC UA <-> DPS. Below those systems, the detector has SC/OPC UA
control/status and DPS/SNMP/EtherCAT equipment paths. Wired protection is
connected through the remote I/O. Bridge-server deployment is outside the main
talk; the source ICD figure is retained in a backup at Manuel's request.

Cryogenics clarification
Cryogenics lives in the CUC. Its controls are divided internally, but to DUNE
it is a single system. Do not turn the internal divisions into three separate
DPS interfaces. Describe process control, critical detector conditions and the
interface; distinguish documented architecture from the run-state agreement
still needed with cryogenics and detector owners.

Interlock review and brain
Introduce the matrix before the detector systems. Review the interlocks already
proposed by each consortium and in the ICDs. Trace condition/source -> physical
input -> controller decision -> actuator/load -> feedback. Use current rack,
parts and power documents to identify coverage and actual additions. Keep
conflicting requirements visible. Shared I/O is counted once.
The brain is a commercial PLC based on BDE: validated conditions and requests,
per-load permits, trip latches, output action and readback. Reset and re-enable
are separate. Required failure responses are defined per function. CUC central
PLC placement remains proposed; no installed location or qualified logic is claimed.

VD hardware scope
Ten BDE groups of eight WIECs, 80 WIECs, 20 PL506 supplies and 480 WIBs.
Existing EtherCAT and dedicated PL506/ISEG inhibits coexist with SNMP control.
Show fan cooling and the possible bias consequence without silently adopting
the historical HD fan-to-bias rule as an approved VD dependency.
HD can later extend the PLC system or use another PLC.

PDS
PDS LV scope remains 34 feeds. DAPHNE control path: DPS -> SC -> OPC UA
interface -> DAPHNE; power branch: DPS -> SNMP -> LV supply -> DAPHNE.
Use the working ICD diagram and include DAQ CCM/opmon and Hermes. A SiPM-bias
inhibit example applies to DAPHNE-controlled bias; cathode power follows PoF.
Software action needs acknowledgement/readback and response-time tests.

PoF clarification confirmed by Manuel
Four laser-voltage readings plus one chassis temperature per box.
80 boxes x (4 V + 1 T) = 400 monitoring readings: 320 voltage, 80 temperature.
This replaces the earlier 8 T + 8 V case for the current presentation. It does
not change the physical laser-driver count or establish controller/module counts.
Compare Opta and Beckhoff PLC options. Inhibit through the PDU; an additional
control/enable signal is desirable if the box exposes one. Do not draw that
optional signal as installed or selected.

CalCI and racks
Separate thermometry, ionization lasers and purity monitors. Instrumentation
is distinct from cryogenic process controls. The PrM/SiPM-bias conflict remains
open: older ICD requests bias off, current Q-001 proposes activity tagging.
Rack infrastructure is its own system: racks, feeds, PDUs, smoke/humidity and
sensor support. Include fitted temperature/leak sensors. Subsystems reference
outlets rather than counting the rack hardware again. TDE has 20 racks and
current PDU control only; retain future crate-status/external-inhibit options.
